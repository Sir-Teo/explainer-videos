## Findings to reconcile

1. **Pinsker prior support.** The displayed Gaussian prior has expected ellipsoid energy C² but is not supported on the ellipsoid. Its exact Bayes-risk equality with the linear minimax risk does not give an exact finite-noise lower bound for all rules on that bounded parameter set. The asymptotic proof needs a slightly smaller prior radius, concentration with sufficient slack, and conditioning/truncation with controlled risk. Concentration of energy around the boundary alone does not make the outside probability vanish. Keep exact linear minimax risk distinct from asymptotic unrestricted minimax risk.
2. **Projection constant.** For alpha=1, asymptotically minimizing N epsilon²+C² N^-2 gives constant 3*2^(-2/3), whereas Pinsker's constant is 3^(1/3)*2^(-2/3). Their ratio is 3^(2/3), about 2.0801, not 1.3–1.5. At the n^-2/3 rate this means a factor three in sample size. The smaller ratio in the numerical upper-bound experiment is risk at one selected truth, not worst-case projection risk. Select the truncation cutoff by its analytic risk before evaluating Monte Carlo risk; minimizing reported simulation risks introduces selection optimism.
3. **Le Cam construction.** `GK.lecam_density_point` asserts membership of a Hölder ball without normalizing the bump's derivative bounds. Its formula also treats two bumps as disjoint without specifying their locations/support. Replace the displayed numerical bound with an explicitly valid pair. One candidate for the C² norm-one density class on R is f0=standard normal and f1=f0+h[b(x/w)-b((x-1)/w)], with b(u)=(1-u²)^3 on |u|<=1 and zero otherwise, 0<w<=1/2, h=(1-phi(0))*w²/6. This bump is C², has |b''|<=6, and the disjoint perturbations preserve mass. Positivity follows because the negative support is within [0.5,1.5] and h<=0.026<phi(1.5). Check the remaining norm bounds explicitly, integrate chi-square against f0, and use a justified product-TV bound. Test normalization, positivity, smoothness membership, divergence and the resulting risk bound independently.
4. **Uniform upper bounds.** A KDE MSE at one normal density is not a uniform upper bound for the smoothness class. A genuine bound for densities with ||f||infinity<=1 and ||f''||infinity<=1 is h^4/4+1/(2 sqrt(pi) n h), by Taylor bias and Gaussian-kernel variance. It can accompany the valid two-point lower bound. The bandwidth 1.06*n^-1/5 is a normal-reference integrated-risk rule, not an exact pointwise-MSE optimum. For the normal density at zero the pointwise AMISE constant is (1/sqrt(2))^(1/5). The exact finite-n normal KDE MSE is available from E[K_h(X)]=phi(0)/sqrt(1+h²) and E[K_h(X)²]=1/(2*pi*h*sqrt(h²+2)).
5. **Assouad construction.** The toolkit substitutes a continuous integrated separation into a finite fixed-design Gaussian KL calculation, without checking the design sum, and omits derivative normalization of the Hölder bumps. Build explicit disjoint tent bumps for Lipschitz smoothness one and polynomial C² bumps for smoothness two. Compute Gaussian KL as one half the exact sum of squared mean-vector differences on the actual design. Use a justified decision-reduction constant (m*separation/8 is a conservative bound for binary 0/1 coordinates), rather than assuming the displayed m*separation/2 factor. Independently verify the construction and finite-design bounds.
6. **Lepski selection.** The helper claims to select the largest bandwidth compatible with all smaller ones, but stops after the first failed candidate. Admissibility need not be monotone. A concrete counterexample is estimates [0,1,3,1.5], SDs [1,1,.9,.8], kappa=2: candidate 2 fails, candidate 3 passes all comparisons, but the helper returns 1. Either implement the advertised largest-admissible rule locally or explicitly describe the sequential variant; regenerate affected examples and Board rows.
7. **Higher criticism calibration.** sqrt(2 log log n) is a leading-order null scale, not an asymptotic 5% critical value. The Board must not call its rejection-rate comparison a failed nominal-5% guarantee from that expression. Keep calibration datasets independent from evaluation datasets, disclose their counts and uncertainty, and distinguish the first-order scale from a level-specific calibration. The current HC calculation can return negative infinity for an all-extreme positive sample after excluding every p-value below 1/n; define and test the intended truncated-CDF statistic, boundary evaluation and numerical underflow handling before using it as a general helper.
8. **Clique test.** For the total-edge statistic, finite-n null and alternative powers are available exactly: under the null E~Binomial(N,1/2), N=n(n-1)/2; with a k-clique E=choose(k,2)+Binomial(N-choose(k,2),1/2). Use a valid discrete 5% cutoff and compare simulated powers with these laws. Statements about polynomial-time impossibility need explicit conjectures/reduction conditions, not just the failure of this one test; scenes 10–12 still need reading.
9. **Sequence-model scope.** Independence of transformed finite-design coefficients needs discrete orthonormality; the current cosine drawing uses approximate quadrature. Brown–Low and Nussbaum equivalences require their smoothness/design/noise and positivity assumptions, appropriate losses, and the density transformation 2*sqrt(f). They do not transfer arbitrary sequence-model constants unchanged to every density/regression problem.
10. **README solution signs.** The normal-TV derivation has its tail-probability subtraction reversed: use P_d(X>d/2)-P_0(X>d/2). The two-point squared-loss reduction needs an explicit hypothesis separation 2*delta to justify an estimation error at least delta on misclassification. The optimal linear rule can have many worst-case boundary allocations, not only a uniquely spread worst truth.
11. **Numerical provenance.** Distinguish actual finite-sample risks, exact linear minimax bounds, rate proxies and uniform worst-case theorems. Add Monte Carlo uncertainty from independent datasets to the Board. Audit every remaining scene before implementing final narration/labels. The random Varshamov–Gilbert packing can be accelerated locally using integer XOR/bit-count while preserving the exact candidate RNG stream and distance criterion; the random greedy construction itself does not inherit a deterministic size guarantee from a fixed trial count.

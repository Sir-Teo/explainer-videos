## Findings to reconcile

1. **Pinsker prior support.** The displayed Gaussian prior has expected ellipsoid energy C² but is not supported on the ellipsoid. Its exact Bayes-risk equality with the linear minimax risk does not give an exact finite-noise lower bound for all rules on that bounded parameter set. The asymptotic proof needs a slightly smaller prior radius, concentration with sufficient slack, and conditioning/truncation with controlled risk. Concentration of energy around the boundary alone does not make the outside probability vanish. Keep exact linear minimax risk distinct from asymptotic unrestricted minimax risk.
2. **Projection constant.** For alpha=1, asymptotically minimizing N epsilon²+C² N^-2 gives constant 3*2^(-2/3), whereas Pinsker's constant is 3^(1/3)*2^(-2/3). Their ratio is 3^(2/3), about 2.0801, not 1.3–1.5. At the n^-2/3 rate this means a factor three in sample size. The smaller ratio in the numerical upper-bound experiment is risk at one selected truth, not worst-case projection risk. Select the truncation cutoff by its analytic risk before evaluating Monte Carlo risk; minimizing reported simulation risks introduces selection optimism.
3. **Le Cam construction.** `GK.lecam_density_point` asserts membership of a Hölder ball without normalizing the bump's derivative bounds. Its formula also treats two bumps as disjoint without specifying their locations/support. Replace the displayed numerical bound with an explicitly valid pair. One candidate for the C² norm-one density class on R is f0=standard normal and f1=f0+h[b(x/w)-b((x-1)/w)], with b(u)=(1-u²)^3 on |u|<=1 and zero otherwise, 0<w<=1/2, h=(1-phi(0))*w²/6. This bump is C², has |b''|<=6, and the disjoint perturbations preserve mass. Positivity follows because the negative support is within [0.5,1.5] and h<=0.026<phi(1.5). Check the remaining norm bounds explicitly, integrate chi-square against f0, and use a justified product-TV bound. Test normalization, positivity, smoothness membership, divergence and the resulting risk bound independently.
4. **Uniform upper bounds.** A KDE MSE at one normal density is not a uniform upper bound for the smoothness class. A genuine bound for densities with ||f||infinity<=1 and ||f''||infinity<=1 is h^4/4+1/(2 sqrt(pi) n h), by Taylor bias and Gaussian-kernel variance. It can accompany the valid two-point lower bound. The bandwidth 1.06*n^-1/5 is a normal-reference integrated-risk rule, not an exact pointwise-MSE optimum. For the normal density at zero the pointwise AMISE constant is (1/sqrt(2))^(1/5). The exact finite-n normal KDE MSE is available from E[K_h(X)]=phi(0)/sqrt(1+h²) and E[K_h(X)²]=1/(2*pi*h*sqrt(h²+2)).
5. **Assouad construction.** The toolkit substitutes a continuous integrated separation into a finite fixed-design Gaussian KL calculation, without checking the design sum, and omits derivative normalization of the Hölder bumps. Build explicit disjoint tent bumps for Lipschitz smoothness one and polynomial C² bumps for smoothness two. Compute Gaussian KL as one half the exact sum of squared mean-vector differences on the actual design. Use a justified decision-reduction constant (m*separation/8 is a conservative bound for binary 0/1 coordinates), rather than assuming the displayed m*separation/2 factor. Independently verify the construction and finite-design bounds.
6. **Lepski selection.** The helper claims to select the largest bandwidth compatible with all smaller ones, but stops after the first failed candidate. Admissibility need not be monotone. A concrete counterexample is estimates [0,1,3,1.5], SDs [1,1,.9,.8], kappa=2: candidate 2 fails, candidate 3 passes all comparisons, but the helper returns 1. Either implement the advertised largest-admissible rule locally or explicitly describe the sequential variant; regenerate affected examples and Board rows.
7. **Higher criticism calibration.** sqrt(2 log log n) is a leading-order null scale, not an asymptotic 5% critical value. The Board must not call its rejection-rate comparison a failed nominal-5% guarantee from that expression. Keep calibration datasets independent from evaluation datasets, disclose their counts and uncertainty, and distinguish the first-order scale from a level-specific calibration. The current HC calculation can return negative infinity for an all-extreme positive sample after excluding every p-value below 1/n; define and test the intended truncated-CDF statistic, boundary evaluation and numerical underflow handling before using it as a general helper.
8. **Clique test.** For the total-edge statistic, finite-n null and alternative powers are available exactly: under the null E~Binomial(N,1/2), N=n(n-1)/2; with a k-clique E=choose(k,2)+Binomial(N-choose(k,2),1/2). Use a valid discrete 5% cutoff and compare simulated powers with these laws. Statements about polynomial-time impossibility need explicit conjectures/reduction conditions, not just the failure of this one test; scenes 10–12 were subsequently read; see the additional findings below.
9. **Sequence-model scope.** Independence of transformed finite-design coefficients needs discrete orthonormality; the current cosine drawing uses approximate quadrature. Brown–Low and Nussbaum equivalences require their smoothness/design/noise and positivity assumptions, appropriate losses, and the density transformation 2*sqrt(f). They do not transfer arbitrary sequence-model constants unchanged to every density/regression problem.
10. **README solution signs.** The normal-TV derivation has its tail-probability subtraction reversed: use P_d(X>d/2)-P_0(X>d/2). The two-point squared-loss reduction needs an explicit hypothesis separation 2*delta to justify an estimation error at least delta on misclassification. The optimal linear rule can have many worst-case boundary allocations, not only a uniquely spread worst truth.
11. **Numerical provenance.** Distinguish actual finite-sample risks, exact linear minimax bounds, rate proxies and uniform worst-case theorems. Add Monte Carlo uncertainty from independent datasets to the Board. Audit every remaining scene before implementing final narration/labels. The random Varshamov–Gilbert packing can be accelerated locally using integer XOR/bit-count while preserving the exact candidate RNG stream and distance criterion; the random greedy construction itself does not inherit a deterministic size guarantee from a fixed trial count.


## Additional findings after completing the source read

12. **Assouad lemma as displayed is false for unrestricted squared-loss estimators.** For one binary coordinate, identical data laws, vertices 0 and 1 and squared loss, the displayed assumptions give delta=1 and the claimed lower bound 1/2. The constant estimator 1/2 has worst risk 1/4. The Hamming-loss lemma applies directly to a vertex-valued decision; reducing general estimation to that decision needs a separation/triangle-inequality factor. A conservative 1/8 bound for integrated squared loss avoids the missing factor. The eight-bump picture also uses height .25 for visibility rather than the asserted unit-class height; label it as an exaggerated schematic or draw the normalized actual construction. Distinguish plus/minus sign vertices from 0/1 alternatives when assigning separation constants.
13. **Entropy fixed points are not universally sharp minimax rates.** The global covering-number recipe in the Fano chapter yields d log(n)/n in a regular d-dimensional family, whereas the sharp rate is ordinarily d/n. Label the global result as a possibly loose bound and explain local entropy/packing for the sharp parametric rate. Donoho–Liu modulus characterizations require the relevant model/convexity/regularity conditions; an arbitrary linear functional over an arbitrary model is not automatically characterized by a single pairwise Hellinger modulus.
14. **Block energy shrinkage is not the classical James–Stein factor.** The implemented factor uses m, not m-2, and includes blocks of dimensions one and two. It is a block moment/empirical-Bayes shrinker, not literally Part 25's dominating James–Stein rule in every block. On dyadic blocks Pinsker weights are not uniformly almost constant: their relative width stays order one, and weights near the cutoff vary substantially. Exact first-order Pinsker adaptation needs carefully refined blocks/tuning, not just a claim that growing dyadic blocks make everything exact. A relative error statement for an oracle shrinkage equal to zero is undefined. State an appropriate absolute error or regular signal regime. Universal thresholding needs the noise level and truncation dimension and can pay logarithmic factors; the lesson currently contradicts itself by promising a constant factor for both methods before showing the soft rule's log loss.
15. **Lepski's displayed theorem versus experiment.** The simulation uses fixed kappa=2.2, while the narration claims kappa must grow as sqrt(log n). A fixed-n Bonferroni correction over J~log n comparisons at fixed familywise alpha scales as sqrt(log J), not automatically sqrt(log n); polynomially small failure probabilities used in risk proofs can justify the latter. State the actual calibration goal. An oracle selected by minimizing the simulated average risk is optimistic; fixed-design Gaussian regression has an exact deterministic bias-squared-plus-variance curve, so use that for the bandwidth oracle. The motorcycle windows use a crude global noise estimate from adjacent differences in heteroskedastic historical data; treat them as exploratory smoothing, not an established uncertainty guarantee or evidence from a single crash.
16. **Detection versus support recovery.** The claim that every individual signal must exceed sqrt(2 log n) to be located, and that r<1 means no individual signal can be located, confuses global detection, familywise thresholding, exact support recovery and almost-full recovery. In the rare/weak normal-means model, one can compare global detection r>rho*(beta) with almost-full recovery requiring r>beta, subject to the precise loss/model. Some large individual signals can be discovered even below the almost-full recovery boundary. State which recovery task is being compared. The boundary r=rho*(beta) and endpoints beta=1/2 or 1 need separate treatment; the theorem shown is for independent, known-variance, positively shifted mixtures and beta strictly between 1/2 and 1. Numerical HC must match the displayed truncation formula, including the p>=1/n restriction or a clearly defined alternative.
17. **Honesty is uniform, not a result at one truth.** Coverage near 95% at the one simulated peak does not prove an undersmoothed interval honest over a class. Dividing an MSE-optimal bandwidth by the fixed constant four also does not make bias/SD tend to zero asymptotically; for that route h_under/h_opt must tend to zero while the effective sample size diverges, with uniform bias control. Low's impossibility concerns particular unrestricted nested classes and adaptation goals. Self-similarity, separation or other extra restrictions can permit adaptation; it does not say that data can never inform smoothness or that every adaptive interval must fail at this one function.
18. **Constructive honest reference.** Consider adding an explicitly bias-aware fixed-bandwidth Board row. For the peak f(x)=sin(2*pi*x)+2*exp(-200*(x-.5)^2), valid global derivative bounds are L1=2*pi+40*exp(-.5) and L2=4*pi^2+800. On the actual fixed design/window, a local-average bias bound is L1*abs(mean(x-x0))+(L2/2)*mean((x-x0)^2). The first term handles finite-grid/asymmetric endpoint effects. With known iid Gaussian noise of variance one, the fixed-bandwidth interval radius bias_bound+normal_975/sqrt(k) gives coverage at least .95 for the stated derivative class. A deterministic bandwidth may minimize that guaranteed radius. A Y-selected bandwidth requires simultaneous coverage or other selection adjustment; merely plugging in the selected bandwidth is not justified. Verify derivative bounds, design moments and finite-Gaussian coverage independently.
19. **Bias-aware intervals and optimal length.** Adding worst-case bias to a two-sided Gaussian radius is a valid conservative construction, but it is not automatically the sharp Armstrong–Kolesar folded-normal critical-value or minimax-length procedure. Do not attribute exact optimality to the simplified additive radius without the relevant theorem. Make finite-sample versus asymptotic honesty explicit.
20. **Computational claims need regime and complexity scope.** Sparse-PCA statements need ||v||2=1, a specified Gaussian/weak-spike regime and the relevant dimension/sparsity/sample-size scalings; not every known efficient method has exactly the displayed k^2 log p requirement in every regime. Planted-clique reductions are conditional average-case hardness evidence under their parameter assumptions. The total-edge test, maximum-degree test and spectral methods have different constants/log factors and consistency requirements. Shading the narrow interval between 2 log2(400) and sqrt(400) does not establish a finite-n hardness threshold. Call those asymptotic scales, not proof that each shaded finite problem lacks a fast test.
21. **Regression complexity scope.** Best-subset prediction, coefficient estimation and support recovery have different design requirements. Restricted eigenvalues affect coefficient-error/rate conclusions. A universal lasso tuning gives s log p/n; matching s log(p/s)/n uniformly needs the appropriate tuning/design regime. Enumerating all supports is one algorithm, not proof that every instance must take exponential time; fixed s gives a polynomial enumeration and branch-and-bound can be fast on some instances. Zhang–Wainwright–Jordan results concern specific hard-design/estimator/complexity conditions, not every poorly conditioned design. Convex relaxations are not universally proved to sit exactly at a computational limit, and a new method should be assessed by its assumptions and theorem rather than declared probably wrong.
22. **Other source/visual reconciliations.** Regular submodels need nonzero pathwise derivative for a nontrivial root-n lower bound; a zero derivative can give a degenerate bound. The prior-average risk of a particular rule is not the optimal Bayes risk until minimizing over rules. The Fano rate display should use s log(ep/s) or restrict s away from p, since s log(p/s) vanishes at s=p. Claims of a worst-case soft-threshold example should distinguish the selected large signal vector from a supremum proof. Sobolev/cosine identities need boundary conditions and the constant coefficient treatment; square-summable Sobolev smoothness is not the same as being smooth everywhere. Source helpers still have the copied Part 47 docstring and gap-bridging clipping. Add local crossing/missing-break-preserving geometry and meaningful initial theorem headings, then inspect all captures. The outro's one-bump density exercise needs a zero-integral perturbation or a compensating bump; a positive bump alone is not a density.

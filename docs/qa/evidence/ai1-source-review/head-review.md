## Concrete corrections

1. **Huffman exercise 3 explains the tie incorrectly, in both languages.** `videos/ai/ai1/README.md:153–157` and `:194–197` say merging the two old 0.2 nodes gives lengths `(1,2,3,4,4)`. It cannot: after merging the old pair, the original 0.1 pair remains a 0.2 subtree and cannot become the depth-4 leaves. Both listed length sets and their 2.2-bit averages are valid; the merge explanation is wrong. Exact valid witnesses, with `(a,b,c,d,e)` probabilities `(0.4,0.2,0.2,0.1,0.1)`:
   - `(2,2,2,3,3)`: merge `d+e→de`, `de+b→deb`, `c+a→ca`, `deb+ca→root`.
   - `(1,2,3,4,4)`: merge `d+e→de`, `de+c→dec`, `b+dec→bdec`, `a+bdec→root`.
   Every step joins two current least weights. The different depths result from the later 0.4 tie. Tiny integer-tenths witnesses are retained in `/workspace/runtime/ai1-source-review/cached-identities.json`. The exercise itself in `s17_outro.py:170` is sound; its solution is in the README, so this correction alone needs no scene change.

2. **The next-token loss is overgeneralized to every language model/training stage.** `videos/ai/ai1/s08_cross_entropy.py:247`, `:264–269`, and the README's chapter-7 summary claim every language model is trained to minimize exactly the left-to-right next-token formula. That is correct for autoregressive next-token pretraining, not all language modeling or post-training. BERT conditions on both sides; RLHF uses an additional reward-based objective. Both appear later in this very series. Suggested scope: “Autoregressive language models are pretrained with next-token cross-entropy.” Keep the expected-cross-entropy floor tied to a fixed data distribution, not a guarantee on any finite empirical loss. This affects both the headline and English/Chinese narration/table entries if changed. Primary evidence: [BERT paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805), abstract; [InstructGPT paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155), abstract. These sources were checked directly.

3. **The historical attribution of the absolute entropy gap to Shannon's redundancy definition is imprecise.** `videos/ai/ai1/s04_entropy.py:355–356` calls the absolute gap `log₂K−H` what Shannon called redundancy; the chapter-3 README summary repeats it. In section 7 of the [1948 primary paper](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~ctm/home/text/others/shannon/entropy/entropy.pdf), reprint p.14, Shannon defines redundancy as **`1−H/Hmax`**, a dimensionless fraction. The plotted gap is still meaningful as absolute redundancy in bits; distinguish those two quantities or remove the precise historical attribution. The relevant Chinese template is `zh_lines.py:168–169` and persisted narration table at `narration_zh.json:206–207`.

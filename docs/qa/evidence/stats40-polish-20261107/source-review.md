Read all seventeen chapters, their common helpers and manifest, README including problems and solutions, the full computation module, survival toolkit, existing toolkit tests and the World cohort generator. The intermediate findings file records discovery notes; this document describes the resolved implementation. This review does not approve Chinese material, listening or narrated masters.

The original delayed-entry example compared a World cohort that resets frailty at recruitment with a different age-40 population. It now generates a separate population from age 40, draws entry independently, retains only survivors to entry, and preserves their survival-selected frailty. Its reference median is 78.349 years, delayed-entry KM gives 78.678 and ignoring entry gives 82.147. The age-60 median for the stated covariates is solved numerically, 18.627 years, rather than rounded from a quarter-year grid. Independent statsmodels risk sets and gamma quadrature verify these changes.

The counting-process compensator now integrates actual risk time exactly. Statements about unbiasedness stop the target when the risk set empties. Greenwood uses orthogonal martingale increments rather than claiming independent nonlinear product factors. Pointwise intervals and Gaussian-process limits are approximate under their stated conditions; the Hall–Wellner discussion no longer uses an incorrect Brownian-bridge clock or a universal width multiplier. Conditional independent censoring is distinguished from the unconditional independence needed by pooled KM.

All six real teaching datasets were independently compared with upstream R objects. Numeric CSV values agree to their rounding precision, and categorical values agree exactly. The 42 Gehan time/event/treatment triples agree exactly. The original 21 pair assignments are retained locally for restricted-mean uncertainty: 3,999 whole-pair bootstrap resamples give a 95% percentile interval of 8.009–21.277 weeks for the 14.621-week difference. Its small-sample and flat-tail convention is disclosed. Standard log-rank and Cox examples are explicitly unstratified teaching calculations.

The Fine–Gray interval now uses 399 patient bootstrap fits, refitting the censoring KM each time, rather than treating inverse conditional information as a full uncertainty estimate. The Brier score uses G(T−) for deaths and right-continuous G(t) for observed survivors, handles absence of censoring and rejects horizons without censoring support. Independent complete-data and censoring-jump examples verify the scoring convention.

The conformal Board retains the actual 200 independent calibrations and their empirical Monte Carlo SE, 0.001715 in coverage units, instead of inventing an equivalent independent-repetition count. Narration and documentation distinguish known censoring information, correct weights and exchangeability from model assumptions; marginal coverage is not a per-patient conditional guarantee.

Other corrections qualify non-significant trial results, distinguish period incidence-rate ratios from instantaneous hazard ratios, disclose the 1,000-patient Cox subsample, label the simplified residual trend diagnostic, correct the integrated shared-frailty likelihood and gamma parameterization, and separate associations from causal effects. Finite survival plateaus do not establish cure, and immortal-time correction does not resolve all confounding. Numerical statements in the README were reconciled with the regenerated inputs.

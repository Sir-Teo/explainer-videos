# Industry, Built: Part 17, Automobiles

Part 17 of a sixty-four-part series on how each industry works (see [the series plan](../README.md) and
[the syllabus](../SYLLABUS.md)), the third of Act III, *Making*. It asks: why is it so hard to make money making the most
desired product of the century? It answers with Henry Ford's own account of the first moving lines (*My Life and Work*,
1922); Toyota's own pages on its plants and its production system; the annual reports of General Motors, Ford, Tesla,
Rivian, Lucid, Stellantis, Ferrari, Toyota and Honda, of the suppliers BorgWarner, Aptiv, Lear, Autoliv, Goodyear and
Magna, and of the dealers AutoNation, Lithia, Penske, CarMax and Carvana; Volkswagen's Annual Report 2025 and BYD's 2025
results; the 2022 Economic Census of car assembly, parts and dealers; the Federal Reserve's capacity utilization since
1948; the BEA's sales and dealers' months of supply; the BLS's price indexes and employment; UN Comtrade's car exports;
the Department of Energy's battery cost estimates; the IEA's electric car shares; China's 2017 and 2020 plans and its
carmakers' association's results; the US and EU tariffs on Chinese electric cars; the EIA's 2017 outlook; and the Works'
plant, platform and showroom duel. The answer: a car plant costs nearly the same full or empty, while demand for cars can
collapse in two years, so profit depends on volume and a launch's upside is capped by the line; every maker is tempted to
fill its own plant, so discounts spread, as in China now; and the carmaker is above all a buyer and assembler of other
companies' work, while much of the money is made around the car, in servicing, lending and a few exclusive brands.

It opens on 2025: Toyota kept 7.6 cents of each dollar of revenue (its year to March 2026), Volkswagen 2.1, General Motors
1.5, while Honda lost 1.9, Ford 4.4 and Stellantis 14.5. Then three arrivals: Ford's line at Highland Park, where a
chassis took 12 hours 28 minutes standing still, 5 hours 50 minutes drawn by a rope and 1 hour 33 minutes on the moving
line of 1914; Toyota's system, built from Sakichi Toyoda's self-stopping loom of 1896 and Kiichiro Toyoda's just in time;
and the electric car and China, from 0.7% of the world's new cars in 2015 to 25% in 2025, and China's car exports from $4bn
to $110bn. From there it covers:

## What is computed, and from what

`compute.py` reads the Filing Room and the Register. Run it, the plant, the platform, the battery and the loan are the
Works' (`explainer/industry/works/auto.py`), with illustrative parameters labelled on screen: in the duel a car's demand is
the plan times (own price / list price) to the power -7 times (rival's price / list price) to the power 6, and each plant's
profit is cars sold up to its capacity times the price less $36,000, less its shifts and fixed costs. Net margins are
profit attributable to the parent over revenue from the companies' filings (Stellantis tags only profit including
non-controlling interests, Ford in some years too), by fiscal year; Toyota's and Honda's years end in March. Volkswagen's,
Ferrari's, Stellantis's, Toyota's, Honda's and BYD's figures are converted at the Federal Reserve's 2025 average rates
(US$1.13 to the euro, ¥149.6 and RMB 7.19 to the dollar). R&D per vehicle divides research and development expense by the
vehicles sold or delivered in the year (Toyota's R&D is for its year to March 2025, the latest in its tagged filings;
Volkswagen's is 6.7% of revenue, from its five-year review; GM's units include its Chinese joint ventures). The dollar of a
car combines AutoNation's gross margin on new cars with the census's materials and payroll shares of the assemblers'
shipments, so it is an estimate. Hours per vehicle divide the census's employees, at 2,000 hours a year, by the Federal
Reserve's light vehicle assemblies for 2022. Capacity utilization, sales and dealers' months of supply are annual
averages of monthly figures; the CPIs are divided by the all-items CPI (the BLS priced new cars only in some months before
1953). The fitted curve is a straight line through the log-odds of the IEA's world shares for 2021-2025.

## Problems and solutions

**1.** *A plant sells cars at $45,000 that cost $36,000 each in parts, materials, freight and warranty. Its two shifts cost
$288m a year each whatever it builds, and its tooling, engineering and overhead $900m. How many cars must it sell to break
even, and what does it make at 220,000?*

*Solution.* Each car leaves $9,000. Fixed costs are 2 × 288 + 900 = $1,476m, so break-even is 1,476m / 9,000 = 164,000
cars, 66% of the 250,000 two shifts can build. At 220,000 cars: 220,000 × 9,000 - 1,476m = $504m.

**2.** *In the showroom duel your profit is -$90m if both hold, +$22m if you discount and the rival holds, -$560m if you
hold and the rival discounts, and -$486m if both discount (the game is symmetric). What will two sensible makers do, and
why is it a dilemma?*

*Solution.* If the rival holds, discounting (+22) beats holding (-90); if the rival discounts, discounting (-486) beats
holding (-560). Discounting is better whatever the rival does, so both discount and each loses $486m, although both would
lose only $90m if both held. Each one's best move leaves both worse off: a prisoner's dilemma.

**3.** *Rivian spent $1.668bn on research and development in 2025 and delivered 42,247 vehicles. What was its R&D per
vehicle, and what would it have been had its plant built its full 215,000?*

*Solution.* 1,668m / 42,247 = $39,482 a vehicle. At 215,000: 1,668m / 215,000 = $7,758, still about eight times Toyota's
$971, which spread its engineering over more than nine million vehicles.

**4.** *AutoNation's gross profit in 2025 was $664.8m on new cars, $462.6m on used cars, $2,355.1m on parts and service and
$1,464.4m on finance and insurance. What share came from new cars, and what share from servicing and finance together?*

*Solution.* The total is $4,948.5m. New cars: 664.8 / 4,948.5 = 13.4%. Parts and service plus finance and insurance:
(2,355.1 + 1,464.4) / 4,948.5 = 77.2%. The dealer sells cars to sell everything that comes after them.

**5.** *The DOE estimates a battery pack cost $1,415 per kWh in 2008 and $139 in 2023 (2023 dollars). What does a 75 kWh pack
cost at the 2023 figure, at what cost per kWh would it match a $6,000 engine, gearbox and exhaust, and when would the
estimate get there if it kept falling at its 2008-2023 average rate?*

*Solution.* 75 × 139 = $10,425. Parity: 6,000 / 75 = $80 per kWh. The average yearly factor is (139 / 1,415)^(1/15) =
0.857, a fall of 14.3% a year; ln(80 / 139) / ln(0.857) = 3.6 years after 2023, so around 2027. But the fall has slowed:
from 2020 to 2023 it averaged 11% a year ((139 / 199)^(1/3) = 0.887), and at that pace parity comes 4.6 years after 2023,
in 2028.

## Glossary

- **Break-even volume:** the output at which a plant's revenue covers its variable costs and its fixed costs.
- **Contribution margin:** price less variable cost; what each extra car adds towards fixed costs and profit.
- **Capacity utilization:** output as a share of what the plants could produce.
- **Platform:** the shared engineering under several models: floor, structure, wiring, electronics.
- **Tier-one supplier:** a company that sells systems directly to carmakers; tier two sells to tier one.
- **Body in white:** a car's welded steel body before it is painted.
- **Jidoka and just-in-time:** the two pillars of the Toyota Production System: stopping when something goes wrong, and
  making only what is needed, when it is needed.
- **Kanban:** the card that tells a step what the step after it has used.
- **Months of supply:** the cars on dealers' lots divided by a month's sales.
- **Franchised dealer:** an independent business licensed to sell and service a carmaker's brand.
- **Residual value:** what a leased car is expected to be worth when it comes back.
- **New energy vehicle:** China's term for battery electric, plug-in hybrid and fuel cell vehicles.
- **Countervailing duty:** a tariff set to offset a foreign government's subsidies.
- **Prisoner's dilemma:** a game in which each player's best move, whatever the other does, leaves both worse off.

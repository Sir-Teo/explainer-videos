# Economics, Built: Part 49, The Economic Approach

Part 49 of a sixty-eight-part course in economics (see [the series plan](../README.md) and
[the syllabus](../SYLLABUS.md)), the seventh of Act IX, *The Fields*. It asks how far the model of choice under
constraint can be pushed into the rest of life. Its answer: far. Penalties, marriages, children, schooling, health and
even addiction respond to prices and constraints, measurably. But the measuring is hard, the chance of being caught
deters more than the length of a sentence, and where a price changes what an act means, as a fine for lateness or a
payment for blood, the simple model breaks until those meanings are put into it.

It opens on a parking space: in the 1960s Gary Becker, late for a student's oral examination at Columbia, weighed a
car park against the chance and size of a fine for parking on the street, parked on the street, and was not
ticketed; on the walk to the room, he said later, it occurred to him that the city had probably made the same kind of
calculation in setting its inspections and fines. *Crime and Punishment: An Economic Approach* appeared in 1968. From
there it covers:

- crime as a choice: 20,000 Island offenders with log-normal gains, caught one time in five and sentenced to three
  years; those indifferent to risk offend when the gain beats the expected punishment of 0.6 years (73% do);
  Becker's planner, for whom catching is costly and fines are not, slides along a curve of equal deterrence to the
  largest fine and the thinnest enforcement (a fine of 40 and a chance of 12%, with 2.5% offending); why real
  penalties are not like that (wealth, prison's cost, Stigler's marginal deterrence, mistakes);
- Play it: double the chance or double the sentence? For risk-neutral offenders it makes no difference (73% to 40%
  either way); for the risk-averse the sentence deters more (7% against 24%); for those who count later years of
  prison for less, the chance (53% against 69%); Nagin's review (certainty deters more consistently than severity)
  and Lee and McCrary's eighteenth birthday in Florida (offending falls about 2%, an elasticity to sentence length no
  more negative than -0.13 for the young);
- deterrence measured: on the Island's 1,000 blocks, where police go where crime is, comparing blocks gives +0.75
  against a planted -0.3; following blocks over time with police counted with error gives -0.08 (only 29% of the
  measured changes are real); moving police at random gives -0.33; Di Tella and Schargrodsky's Buenos Aires (car
  thefts down 75% on protected blocks, nothing a block away) and Draca, Machin and Witt's London (-0.3 to -0.4); the
  1950 murder rates of 44 states, where executions seem to raise murder alone (+8, s.e. 5) and shrink to +2 (s.e. 10)
  with region, income and race, Leamer's and McManus's lesson;
- the household as a small factory (Becker 1965): full income and the time price of commodities; on the Island, a
  wife with the comparative advantage at home starts paid work when her wage reaches 0.7 of her husband's; women's
  labour force participation in the US, 33% in 1948, 60% at the peak in 1999, 57% in 2024 (men's 87% to 68%);
  Goldin;
- marriage as a matching market: Becker's complementarity and sorting; a Choo-Siow market by schooling on the Island,
  with like marrying like 1.41 times as often as chance, the surplus of each pairing read back from one census; a
  fifth more men (married men 82% to 74%, women 78% to 85%); more women than men with degrees (single graduate women
  22% to 27%);
- children: Becker and Lewis's quantity and quality (as the mother's wage rises from 0.1 to 3, children fall from 5.5
  to 1.4); children per woman since 1950 (the world 4.9 to 2.3; China 6.1 in 1970 and 2.7 in 1979, before the
  one-child policy, 1.0 in 2023; South Korea 0.72); Angrist and Evans's same-sex siblings on AER's 30,000 mothers from
  the 1980 census (a third child 6.7 points more likely, 0.40 weeks less work, a Wald estimate of -6.0 weeks with a
  standard error of 3.8); Goldin and Katz's pill;
- bargaining inside the household: the unitary and collective models; on the Island, moving a tenth of income to the
  mother raises child spending 13%; Lundberg, Pollak and Wales's UK child benefit;
- an intermission of the world's children per woman since 1950, drawn year by year and played as a tune;
- schools: California's 420 districts (one pupil more per teacher, -2.28 points; -1.00 with the share learning
  English and on free lunch); Tennessee's STAR (5,786 kindergarteners in 79 schools: small classes +5.6 percentile
  points within schools, s.e. 1.4; aides +0.3; larger for pupils on free lunch and for black pupils); Maimonides' rule
  on the Island (naive -0.003, the discontinuity -0.021 against a planted -0.020) and in Israel (gains in 1991, none in
  2002-11, with bunching at the cutoffs); teachers' value added (raw spread 0.16 against a true 0.12, reliability
  0.62, shrunk 0.10; the move test 1.01, or 0.38 when schools' pupils differ and the estimate ignores it; Chetty,
  Friedman and Rockoff's 0.14 and 0.10);
- early childhood: Perry Preschool (123 children from 1962, a 7-10% return), the Heckman curve and Rea and Burton's
  test of it, Head Start's trial and Tennessee's pre-K trial;
- the demand for health: Grossman's health capital (the Island's death age 86, or 90 for someone better at producing
  health); the Australian Health Survey of 1977-78 (private cover, 13% more visits with an interval from 6% fewer to
  36% more); Gruber and Owings's caesareans; Cooper et al.'s 12% monopoly premium for hospitals;
- drugs, patents and despair: an Island drug ($55 under the patent against a cost of $10; a profit of $2.0 million a
  year and $1.0 million of treatment not given); Case and Deaton's deaths of despair; Alpert et al.'s triplicate
  states (OxyContin 50% lower; 34% fewer drug and 45% fewer opioid overdose deaths a year);
- addiction: Becker and Murphy's rational addict cuts back 7% when a price rise is announced, before it bites, while
  a myopic one keeps rising; cigarettes in the 48 states (least squares -1.21; the sales tax as instrument -1.08;
  ten-year changes with both taxes -1.20, as in Stock and Watson); Gruber and Kőszegi;
- the critics: Lazear's economic imperialism; Gneezy and Rustichini's day-care fine; Mellström and Johannesson's blood
  donors (52% to 30% for women when paid); Frey and Oberholzer-Gee's Swiss villages; Bénabou and Tirole's reply;
- three dials (the elasticity of crime to police; the effect of a smaller class; the spread of teachers' effects),
  practice, *Called it?* on DiIulio's super-predators (wrong) and a frontier of very low fertility, the new relation
  between women's work and fertility, and sex ratios and China's saving.

## What is computed, and from what

Everything plotted is computed by `compute.py` from Agora's economic approach (`explainer.economics.agora.family`)
with the Island's Part 49 truths, or from the data named above; the narration states the cached numbers.

- **Offenders.** 20,000 gains, log-normal with median 1 (a year of prison) and log-sd 0.8; offend when (1 - p) u(g) +
  p u(g - F) > 0 with CARA utility (coefficient 0 or 0.5) and a felt punishment that counts each later year of a
  sentence beta times the one before (beta 1 or 0.75).
- **Becker's planner.** Harm 2 per offence plus 1 per unit of the chance of catching, over a grid of chances and fines
  up to 40, for risk-neutral offenders.
- **Police.** 1,000 blocks with a log propensity to crime; log police = 1 + 0.8 x propensity + noise; log crime =
  2 + propensity - 0.3 x log police + noise; police counted with error of s.d. 0.35; a random 40% increase on half
  the blocks.
- **Murder, 1950.** Least squares of the murder rate on convictions per murder and executions per conviction, alone
  and with southern, income and the non-white share, with robust standard errors.
- **The household.** Utility 0.6 log C + 0.4 log Z, with C = 1 (1 - t1) + w (1 - t2) and Z = 0.6 t1 + 1.0 t2, solved
  on a grid for each wage w.
- **Marriage.** Choo and Siow's equilibrium for three types a side (men 3,000, 2,600 and 1,800; women 2,600, 2,800 and
  2,400) and a planted surplus matrix, by iterating on the numbers who stay single; one census drawn as a multinomial
  sample; the surplus estimated as 2 log(mu_ij / sqrt(mu_i0 mu_0j)).
- **Quantity and quality.** log c + 0.3 log n + 0.15 log q with c + n (pi + q) = 3 + w and pi = 0.05 + 0.15 w, solved
  exactly.
- **Same-sex siblings.** AER's subsample of 30,000 of Angrist and Evans's mothers; the first stage, the reduced form
  and the Wald estimate with a delta-method standard error.
- **Bargaining.** A child good valued 0.15 by the father and 0.40 by the mother; the mother's weight a logistic
  function of her share of income (slope 1.5) or a half.
- **STAR.** Kindergarten reading and maths scores as percentiles of the regular classes' distributions, averaged;
  small-class and aide dummies after removing school means, errors clustered by school.
- **California.** The average of reading and maths on pupils per teacher, alone and with the shares learning English
  and on reduced-price lunch.
- **Maimonides.** 2,000 Island schools with 20-160 pupils in a grade, classes by the rule plus noise, scores falling
  0.02 s.d. per pupil and rising with an advantage correlated with enrolment; two-stage least squares within 15 of the
  cutoffs with a linear control.
- **Teachers.** 1,200 teachers with value added of s.d. 0.12, classes of 25 (pupil noise 0.55, class shocks 0.08);
  value added from the first two years, shrunk by its reliability; in the fourth year half the teachers move, and the
  change in a school's mean score is regressed on the change in its teachers' mean predicted value added.
- **Health.** H* = 0.3 / (r + delta), r = 3%, delta 2.5% at 20 growing 5.9% a year; life ends below 0.25.
- **Doctor visits.** A Poisson regression of visits on insurance, illness, reduced activity, a health score, sex, age
  and its square, income and chronic conditions, with sandwich standard errors.
- **The drug.** Demand q = 100 - p, cost 10.
- **Cigarettes.** Logs of packs a head and the real price including sales tax; 2SLS with the real general sales tax
  (and, in ten-year differences, the real cigarette tax) as instruments, controlling for real income in the
  differences; heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors and first-stage F.
- **Addiction.** u = 2c - c^2/2 + 0.2 c S - 0.1 S^2/2 - p c, S' = 0.7 S + c, beta 0.95; the rational path by backward
  induction on the quadratic value function, the myopic path c = 2 - p + 0.2 S.

## Problems and solutions

**1.** *Deterrence: potential offenders' gains are uniform on [0, 10], the chance of being caught is 0.2 and the
sentence 5. What share of risk-neutral offenders offend? With the chance doubled? With the sentence doubled?*

*Solution.* The expected punishment is 0.2 x 5 = 1, so those with gains above 1 offend: 90%. Doubling either the
chance or the sentence doubles the expected punishment to 2, and 80% offend. For a risk-neutral offender only the
product matters; a risk-averse one is deterred more by the longer sentence, and one who discounts later years by the
higher chance.

**2.** *Becker's planner: with those gains, harm 1 per offence, catching costing p per potential offender and fines
up to 20, show that the fine should be the largest and find the best p.*

*Solution.* With p F = k the share offending is 1 - k/10, whatever the mix, and catching costs p = k/F, which falls
as F rises: for any level of deterrence the largest fine is cheapest, so F = 20. Then the loss is (1 - 2p) + p =
1 - p for p up to 0.5, falling in p; at p = 0.5 nobody offends and the loss is 0.5, and a higher p only costs more.
The best plan is F = 20 and p = 0.5, full deterrence, because each offence does more harm (1) than the cost of the
extra catching needed to prevent it.

**3.** *The household: he earns 20 an hour, she 15; he makes 1 unit of home output an hour, she 2. Who has the
comparative advantage at home, and what does it cost to make 10 units each way?*

*Solution.* A unit of home output costs him 20 of forgone earnings and her 15/2 = 7.5: she has the comparative
advantage at home, although he earns more. Ten units cost 5 of her hours, 75, or 10 of his, 200. The household is
125 better off with her doing it, whatever anyone thinks about who should.

**4.** *Choo and Siow: 400 marriages between two types, 100 single men and 64 single women of those types. What is
the surplus Phi?*

*Solution.* Phi = 2 log(400 / sqrt(100 x 64)) = 2 log(400/80) = 2 log 5 = 3.22. The more marriages relative to the
singles on both sides, the larger the gain the market reveals.

**5.** *Same-sex siblings: the first stage is 0.067 and the reduced form -0.40 weeks. Find the Wald estimate, and say
what it measures and for whom.*

*Solution.* -0.40/0.067 = -6.0 weeks of work a year. It is the effect of a third child on its mother's work, for the
mothers who had a third child because their first two were of the same sex (the compliers): a local average
treatment effect, not the effect for every mother.

**6.** *Quantity and quality: with a = 0.3, b = 0.15, full income 4 and pi = 0.2, find c, n and q. What happens to q
if pi doubles?*

*Solution.* c = Y/(1 + a) = 3.08; n pi = (a - b) Y/(1 + a) = 0.46, so n = 2.31; n q = b Y/(1 + a) = 0.46, so q = 0.2,
which is b pi/(a - b). If pi doubles to 0.4, q doubles to 0.4 and n halves to 1.15: dearer children are fewer and
better provided for.

**7.** *Value added: estimates from two years have a spread of 0.156; one year's noise variance is 0.0185. Find the
reliability, and shrink a raw estimate of +0.3.*

*Solution.* The noise in a two-year mean has variance 0.0185/2 = 0.00925; the estimates' variance is 0.156^2 =
0.0243, so the signal is 0.0151 and the reliability 0.0151/0.0243 = 0.62. The best prediction of the teacher's true
effect is 0.62 x 0.3 = 0.19 above the average.

**8.** *Rational addiction in two periods: u = a c1 - c1^2/2 + beta [a c2 - c2^2/2 + gamma c1 c2] - p1 c1 - beta
p2 c2. Show that an announced rise in p2 lowers c1 when gamma > 0.*

*Solution.* The first-order condition for c2 gives c2 = a - p2 + gamma c1. For c1: a - c1 + beta gamma c2 - p1 = 0.
Substituting, c1 (1 - beta gamma^2) = a - p1 + beta gamma (a - p2), so dc1/dp2 = -beta gamma / (1 - beta gamma^2),
which is negative when gamma > 0 (and beta gamma^2 < 1). With adjacent complementarity, consuming now raises the
value of consuming later; a dearer future makes present use less worthwhile, so the rational addict cuts back before
the price rises. A myopic one, who ignores the second period, does not.

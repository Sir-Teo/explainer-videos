# Economics, Built: Part 46, Development

Part 46 of a sixty-eight-part course in economics (see [the series plan](../README.md) and
[the syllabus](../SYLLABUS.md)), the fourth of Act IX, *The Fields*. It asks why the poor stay poor, and what actually
helps. Its answer: sometimes because a threshold keeps them there, and a large enough push frees them; more often
because the markets for credit, insurance and information work worst for the poor, and small frictions loom large.
What helps is growth above all, and programmes whose effects have been measured, whose money is spent where the next
dollar does most, and whose results are checked again where they are used.

It opens on Cohen and Dupas's bed nets: at 20 Kenyan prenatal clinics the price of an insecticide-treated net was drawn
at random, from free to 40 shillings (about 60 cents); demand fell by about 60% at the top price, and women who paid
were no more likely to use their nets. The Island's clinic repeats it: 95% take a free net and 36% one at 60 cents (a
fall of 62%), and 65% of takers sleep under it at every price; if paying had screened for use, the share would have
risen from 71% to 88%. From there it covers:

- counting the poor: the World Bank's recipe (national lines, purchasing-power parity, one world line, surveys); the
  line's re-sets from about $1 a day (1990) to $3.00 at 2021 prices (June 2025); Chen and Ravallion's 2008 revision (1.4
  billion poor in 2005, about 400 million more than the old prices said); the World Bank's own headcounts at $3.00 a day,
  from 47.1% of the world in 1981 to 10.1% in 2024, East Asia from 77% to 2%, South Asia from 56% to 2.6%, sub-Saharan
  Africa from 61.5% (1990) to 45%; China from 97% (1981) to under 1% by 2018 and India from 50.9% (1983) to 2.6% (2023);
  Deaton's warnings about whose prices and which surveys;
- a game: ten million dollars and six Island programmes with diminishing returns; all on the programme with the best
  first million (bed nets) buys 5.0 thousand healthy years, equal shares 18.6, the best split 19.8, which funds five
  programmes until the last million buys about 1.23 in each and gives microcredit (0.8 at its first dollar) nothing;
  GiveWell's room for more funding;
- poverty traps: a household that chooses between a hoe (sqrt k) and a cow with a fixed cost (4 sqrt k - 11), saving 30%
  and losing 10% a year, has an S-shaped capital map with steady states at 9 and 59.8 and a threshold at 18.2; of 3,000
  Island households, 9% start above it and 9% are above it after twelve years; a one-off transfer of 10 pushes 67%
  across and leaves 59% above it, a transfer of 20 pushes everyone across and leaves 97%; Kraay and McKenzie's review;
  Balboni et al.'s Bangladesh threshold;
- the dual economy: Lewis's surplus labour, with the Island's modern wage flat at subsistence until year 28 and rising
  to 2.5 times it by year 60; the raw agricultural productivity gap recomputed for 172 countries from the World Bank's
  latest data (a median of 2.8 in the poorest quarter of countries, 1.7 in the richest); Gollin, Lagakos and Waugh;
- credit: the lender's break-even rate, 53% a year on the Island (10 points the cost of funds, 19 defaults, 24
  screening and collection), and Aleem's Pakistani moneylenders at about 78%; group lending; a randomized Island
  microlender (32% take-up; profits +4.0, s.e. 0.8, on a mean of 218; +12.4 per borrower) whose quantile effects are
  zero over most of the distribution and about +29 at the 95th percentile; Banerjee, Karlan and Zinman's six trials;
  Meager's pooling; the returns to grants in Sri Lanka;
- cash: PROGRESA's design and Schultz's findings; GiveDirectly and Haushofer and Shapiro ($158 to $193 a month); the
  village multiplier, 1/(1 - 0.75 x 0.8) = 2.5 (2.47 with a little inflation), and Egger et al.'s measured 2.5; Uruguay's
  PANES households (1,948 near the cutoff): support for the government jumps by 10 points (s.e. 3) at the eligibility
  cutoff, by local linear regression, against the authors' 11 to 14;
- the graduation programme: BRAC's package; six trials, consumption higher in five; the Island's households given 10,
  whose median change over twelve years is about -2 below the threshold and +1 to +2 above it;
- an intermission of the share of the world below $3.00 a day, 1981-2024;
- schools: Duflo's Indonesian schools by difference in differences; the Island's version, 0.155 more years and 1.48%
  higher wages per school, a Wald ratio of 0.095 against a planted 0.08; learning poverty (70% in 2022); Teaching at the
  Right Level (0.15 and 0.70 standard deviations);
- health: deworming and its spillovers (on the Island, treated pupils miss 6.7 points fewer days than untreated
  classmates, who miss 4.3 fewer than pupils in control schools; whole schools differ by 9.3 points); the worm wars;
  Thornton's Malawi data (2,834 people: 34% collected their HIV results with no incentive, 79% with any, 64% with less
  than a quarter of a dollar; 74% within a kilometre of the centre, 65% three to five kilometres away);
- risk: Townsend's test, which on the Island recovers one minus the pooled share exactly (0.15 at 85% pooling) while
  the regression without village-year effects gives 0.25; index insurance and basis risk (a farmer's best cover falls
  from 0.40 to nothing as basis risk rises to one half, with a 30% loading and 80% trust); Cai, de Janvry and Sadoulet's
  1,410 households, where a default of buying raised take-up from 41% to 52% (s.e. 2.6 points);
- farms, phones and firms: Duflo, Kremer and Robinson's fertilizer offers; the Island's present-biased farmers (24% use
  fertilizer, 35% with free delivery offered at harvest, 24% with the same offer at planting, 37% at half price at
  planting, and 39% if all were patient); M-PESA; management in Indian textile plants;
- history: Dell's mita boundary and the Island's version (a jump of -0.28 in log consumption against a planted -0.25);
  Nunn's slave trades and the origins of mistrust;
- the debate over experiments: the 2019 prize; Deaton's objections; twelve Island sites with a true spread of effects
  of 0.12, whose next site's effect could be anything from -0.06 to +0.42; Vivalt; China's fall in poverty, which no
  experiment produced;
- three dials (the return to capital in small firms; the consumption effect of a cash transfer; the return to
  schooling, with Duflo's published range and the Island's estimate added); practice (evaluators, development banks,
  GiveWell's $5,500 per life saved); *Called it?* on Sachs's 2005 forecast that extreme poverty could end by 2025
  (wrong); and a frontier chapter on experiments at scale, poverty maps from satellites and phones, and Togo's Novissi,
  with the Island's targeting rules missing 70% (at random), 52% (by village), 48% (by phone records) and 33% (by a
  full survey) of the poorest 30%.

## What is computed, and from what

Everything plotted is computed by `compute.py` from Agora's village (`explainer.economics.agora.village`) with the
Island's Part 46 truths, or from the data named above; the narration states the cached numbers.

- **Nets.** Willingness to pay lognormal around 42 cents (sigma 1.2), 5% who would refuse a free net, 65% of holders
  using it whatever they paid; prices 0, 15, 22, 30 and 60 cents.
- **The aid budget.** Benefit a s (1 - exp(-x/s)) for each programme, spent in steps of $50,000 on the largest marginal
  benefit (the greedy rule is optimal because each benefit is concave).
- **The trap.** Output max(sqrt k, 4 sqrt k - 11); k' = 0.3 f(k) + 0.9 k with lognormal shocks of 8%; steady states as
  the roots of k' - k; 3,000 households starting lognormal around 10.
- **Lewis.** A modern sector a K^0.4 L^0.6 hiring at a subsistence wage while 60 of 100 workers are surplus, profits
  reinvested; past the turning point the wage rises with the farm's marginal product.
- **The agricultural gap.** ((1 - v)/(1 - e)) / (v/e) with v and e agriculture's shares of value added and employment,
  for each country's latest year from 2015 with all three series.
- **Credit.** (1 + i)(1 - p) = 1 + r + m; the microlender's trial with 40,000 would-be borrowers, a bootstrap band of
  200 resamples for the quantile effects.
- **Cash.** The geometric series of local spending; PANES by separate least-squares lines on each side of the cutoff
  over the extract's whole window (scores within 0.02), with heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors.
- **Schools.** 280 regions, two cohorts, 800 children in each cell; the slopes of the young-minus-old differences on
  school intensity, and their ratio.
- **Health.** 75 schools of 200 pupils, half treated; 75% take the pill and 90% of them are cured; untreated pupils'
  infection falls by half the cured share; absence 15% plus 25 points if infected.
- **Risk.** Three villages of 120 households over ten years; the within-village-year slope and the raw slope; index
  insurance by expected CRRA utility (gamma 2) over cover from 0 to 1.
- **Fertilizer.** Values lognormal around 1.1 times the price, hassle exponential with mean 0.15, a cost of 0.35 for
  buying when cash is short, beta uniform on [0.7, 1], 70% of farmers naive about their future selves.
- **The boundary, the sites, targeting.** A local linear discontinuity within 50 km; twelve sites with true effects
  normal(0.2, 0.15) and standard errors of 0.05, the method-of-moments spread and a predictive interval; four
  targeting rules on 60,000 people in 600 villages.

## Problems and solutions

**1.** *The line: a household of five spends 360 rupees a day; the PPP rate is 22 rupees per international dollar. Is
it below $3.00 a head? What if the poor face prices 20% above the national average?*

*Solution.* 360 / 22 = 16.36 international dollars a day, or 3.27 a head: above the line. If the goods the poor buy
cost 20% more than the national basket, the right rate for them is 22 x 1.2 = 26.4 rupees per dollar, and the same
spending is 360 / 26.4 / 5 = 2.73 a head: below it. Whose prices are used decides whether this household is counted.

**2.** *The trap: f(k) = max(sqrt k, 4 sqrt k - 11), s = 0.3, delta = 0.1. Find the three steady states, and say which
are stable.*

*Solution.* A steady state has s f(k) = delta k. On the hoe, 0.3 sqrt k = 0.1 k gives sqrt k = 3, k = 9; the cow pays
only above (11/3)^2 = 13.4, so the hoe is indeed chosen there. On the cow, with x = sqrt k, 0.3(4x - 11) = 0.1 x^2,
that is x^2 - 12x + 33 = 0, so x = 6 -/+ sqrt 3 = 4.27 or 7.73, and k = 18.2 or 59.8, both above 13.4. The slope of
the map is s f'(k) + 1 - delta: at 9 it is 0.3/6 + 0.9 = 0.95, at 18.2 it is 0.3 x 2/4.27 + 0.9 = 1.04, at 59.8 it is
0.3 x 2/7.73 + 0.9 = 0.98. A slope below one is stable, so 9 and 59.8 are stable and 18.2, the threshold, is not.

**3.** *The moneylender: funds cost 10%, 20% of loans default, screening costs 10% of each loan. Find the break-even
rate. What does a fall in defaults to 10% do?*

*Solution.* (1 + i)(1 - 0.2) = 1 + 0.1 + 0.1 = 1.2, so 1 + i = 1.5 and i = 50%. With 10% defaults, 1 + i = 1.2/0.9 =
1.333, i = 33%. Most of the rate is the cost of default and of information, so whatever lowers them (collateral,
groups that screen their members, credit records) lowers the price of credit more than cheaper funds would.

**4.** *The multiplier: 60% of spending stays in the village, and sellers spend 75% of what they earn. Find the
multiplier. What if the transfer itself is half saved?*

*Solution.* Each round passes on a = 0.6 x 0.75 = 0.45 of the last, so the transfer and the incomes it creates sum to
1/(1 - 0.45) = 1.82 per dollar. If the recipient spends only half, the first round of local income is 0.5 x 0.6 = 0.3
and later rounds shrink by 0.45 each, so the total is 1 + 0.3/(1 - 0.45) = 1.55. Saving is not lost (it may be spent
later, or lent), but it does not multiply within the year.

**5.** *Townsend: with village shocks v and own shocks e, show that the regression of the change in consumption on the
change in income without village-year effects is biased toward one.*

*Solution.* Let income be y = v + e and consumption c = v + (1 - pi) e, where pi is the share of own shocks the village
pools; v cannot be pooled locally. With v and e independent, the slope is cov(c, y)/var(y) = (var v + (1 - pi) var e) /
(var v + var e), a weighted average of 1 (with weight var v) and 1 - pi (with weight var e). It exceeds 1 - pi whenever
var v > 0, and approaches one when village shocks dominate. With the Island's standard deviations, 0.15 and 0.35, and
pi = 0.85 it is 0.28; the village-year effects remove v and leave 1 - pi = 0.15.

**6.** *Duflo: each school per 1,000 children adds 0.15 years and 1.2% to wages. Find the return to a year of school.
What must be true for this to be the return?*

*Solution.* 0.012 / 0.15 = 0.08, an 8% return. It is the return only if schools raised wages through years of school
and nothing else (the exclusion restriction), if young and old cohorts would have changed alike in high- and
low-intensity regions without the programme (parallel trends), and if no child was schooled less because of it; and it
is the return for the children the programme moved, who may differ from the average child.

**7.** *Worms: in treated schools, treated pupils miss 7 points fewer days than untreated ones, who miss 4 fewer than
pupils in control schools. Find the effect on a treated child.*

*Solution.* Against a child in a control school, a treated child misses 7 + 4 = 11 points fewer days: 7 from the pill
directly and 4 from living among treated classmates. The within-school comparison shows only the 7, because its
comparison group is itself treated by the spillover; randomizing whole schools measures the total, and the spillover
separately.

**8.** *The budget: two programmes, B_i(x) = a_i s_i (1 - e^(-x/s_i)), and a budget M. Derive the best split, and the
condition under which one programme gets nothing.*

*Solution.* The marginal benefit of programme i is a_i e^(-x_i/s_i). An interior optimum equates them: ln a_1 - x_1/s_1
= ln a_2 - (M - x_1)/s_2, so x_1 = s_1 s_2 (ln(a_1/a_2) + M/s_2) / (s_1 + s_2), and x_2 = M - x_1. If that formula gives
x_1 >= M, the second programme gets nothing: that happens when a_1 e^(-M/s_1) >= a_2, when the first programme's last
dollar still beats the second's first. On the Island, microcredit's first million buys 0.8 while every funded
programme's last million buys about 1.23, so it is left out.
